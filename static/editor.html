A .tctp is the whole chapter in one file: the pages, every box, the Japanese and the English, the layouts, your paint and hand-cleaned plates, the custom box types and the faces it typesets in. Open one anywhere and it is exactly as you left it.

It carries your settings too, and that includes any API keys saved in this project — treat the file the way you would treat the keys.

The story context beneath it is the other file: a .tct holding the synopsis, the characters, the places and terms and how this series is set — the part that carries into chapter two.

Story settings

The synopsis, the character sheet and the glossary are what keep chapter 4 calling her the same thing chapter 3 did. They travel with every page, and the AI adds to them as it reads. Some projects want none of that — a one-shot, a gag strip, a script somebody else already wrote.

Off: nothing from Synopsis, Characters or Places & terms is sent to the model, and nothing is added to them. What you have written stays on disk.

Each one on its own. A series with a big cast wants the character sheet and not the glossary; somebody working from a script wants the words and no speaker guessed at all.

Language & direction

A webtoon is drawn as one long strip and served as tiles cut every so many pixels — through balloons, through faces, through the middle of the typesetting. When a chapter arrives looking like that it is joined back up and cut again in the gaps between panels. Turn this off to keep the tiles exactly as they came.

Pages come out near the first number, cut at the nearest gap. Where there is no gap before the second one the page is cut at the quietest row there is, and you are told which pages those were.

Detection & OCR

Translation engine

One key per service, not one per step. A key is a fact about the provider — the same Google key that reads the page translates it — and three boxes meant typing it twice and never being sure which of the two a run would use.

Each step calls its own model, and each one is priced on what that model really costs — so reading every page on a cheap model and checking it once on a good one is the difference between a chapter costing thirty coins and three hundred. The menus list what this app can price; anything else is charged at the highest rate and says so.
